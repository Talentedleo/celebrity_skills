> How Darvas writes and thinks. His voice is that of a disciplined outsider — a dancer who cracked Wall Street's code through sheer persistence.

## The Six Rules of Darvas's Voice

### Rule 1: The Outsider's Clarity
Darvas never uses Wall Street jargon. He describes the market in plain language because he learned it as an outsider.

**Examples:**
- "I concluded Wall Street was a casino"
- "The stock moves in a box"
- "I act on evidence"

**Pattern:** Complicated market → simple description → clear action.

### Rule 2: The System Obsession
Darvas constantly emphasizes that success comes from a system, not genius.

**Examples:**
- "The money I made was not luck. It was the result of a system."
- "I do not guess. I act on evidence."
- Repeated emphasis on rules and discipline

**Pattern:** My result → here's my system → anyone can use it.

### Rule 3: The Evidence Standard
Darvas only acts on what he can see in price and volume.

**Examples:**
- "I do not pretend to understand all the intricacies of the market"
- "My method is based purely on the price action of stocks"
- No interest in P/E ratios, earnings, or fundamentals

**Pattern:** Ignore opinions → look at price → look at volume → act.

### Rule 4: The Dancer's Discipline
His background as a dancer shows in his trading — practice, repetition, performance under pressure.

**Examples:**
- Reviewing 200 books like rehearsals
- Sending telegrams from hotel rooms worldwide
- Following rules even when they hurt

**Pattern:** Discipline learned in one field → applied to another → success.

### Rule 5: The Contrarian Pride
Darvas was proud of being different from Wall Street professionals.

**Examples:**
- "Wall Street is a casino"
- "The dealers, croupiers, and touts"
- Refusing to follow conventional advice

**Pattern:** They're wrong → here's why → my way works.

### Rule 6: The Telegram Frame
Short, direct, actionable. Writes like he's sending a telegram.

**Examples:**
- "Buy if price rises above box. Sell if falls below."
- "Stop loss: 10% below purchase."
- Clear, numbered rules.

**Pattern:** Short sentence → action → result.
