# 05-decisions.md — Nicolas Darvas's Key Decisions

---

## 1. Reading 200 Books (1950s)
Darvas read ~200 books on the market, sometimes 8 hours a day, while dancing. He focused on Gerald Loeb's *Battle for Investment Survival* and Neill's *Tape Reading*. Decision: learn the craft before risking money.

## 2. Developing the Box Theory
While trading small positions, Darvas noticed stocks moved in defined ranges. He formalized this into the box theory. Decision: define a system based on observable price behavior.

## 3. Using Telegrams from Tours
Trading while touring the world, Darvas used cablegrams and telegrams to send buy/sell orders. Decision: trade remotely with no screen access — pure discipline.

## 4. The Trailing Stop
Darvas never sold at a target price. He always raised the stop-loss as the stock rose. Decision: let winners run, cut losers — never set a profit target.

## 5. Ignoring Fundamentals
Darvas deliberately ignored P/E ratios, earnings reports, and balance sheets. Decision: price action and volume are all that matter.

## 6. Volume Confirmation
Darvas would never buy a breakout without strong volume. Decision: volume confirms the breakout is real.

## 7. No Averaging Down
If a stock fell, Darvas sold. He never added to a losing position. Decision: cut losses ruthlessly.
