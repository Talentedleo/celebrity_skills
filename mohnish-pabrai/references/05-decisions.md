# 05-decisions.md — Mohnish Pabrai's Key Decisions

---

## 1. Founding TransTech Inc. (1991)

**Context:** Pabrai had $30,000 in his 401(k) and $70,000 in credit card debt.

**The Decision:** Used $30k from 401(k) and maxed out credit cards to start TransTech, an IT consulting company.

**The Logic:**
- Believed in his ability to build a services business
- Knew the IT consulting industry well
- Took calculated risk with personal capital

**The Result:**
- Company grew to $20M+ revenue
- Sold to Kurt Salmon Associates in 2000 for ~$20M
- Provided the capital for his investing career

---

## 2. Founding Pabrai Investment Funds (1999)

**Context:** After selling TransTech, Pabrai had capital and wanted to invest full-time.

**The Decision:** Launched Pabrai Investment Funds, cloning Buffett's partnership structure.

**The Logic:**
- Cloned Buffett's fee model: no management fee, 25% of profits above 6% hurdle
- Focused on concentrated value investing
- Openly modeled after Buffett's early partnerships

**The Result:**
- Fund returned 517% net vs. 43% for S&P 500 over key period
- Pabrai became a respected value investor
- Inspired other "Buffett clone" funds

---

## 3. The Buffett Lunch (2008)

**Context:** Pabrai and Guy Spier wanted to learn from the master.

**The Decision:** Paid $650,100 for a charity lunch with Warren Buffett.

**The Logic:**
- "The best money I ever spent on education"
- Wanted to understand Buffett's thinking directly
- The lunch reinforced his cloning philosophy

**The Result:**
- Three-hour lunch deeply influenced both investors
- Became a legendary story in value investing
- Demonstrated Pabrai's commitment to learning

---

## 4. The Horsehead Investment (Mistake)

**Context:** Pabrai invested in Horsehead Holding Corp, a zinc producer.

**The Decision:** Made a large concentrated bet on a commodity business.

**The Mistake:**
- Zinc is a commodity — no moat, no pricing power
- Underestimated competitive threats from Chinese producers
- Violated multiple checklist items

**The Result:**
- Significant losses
- Pabrai discussed it openly as a learning experience
- Strengthened his commitment to the checklist

---

## 5. Adopting the Investment Checklist

**Context:** After reading The Checklist Manifesto and inspired by Munger.

**The Decision:** Created a formal 10-question investment checklist used before every investment.

**The Logic:**
- Prevents emotional decision-making
- Forces systematic thinking
- Catches mistakes before capital is committed

**The Result:**
- Became a hallmark of his investment process
- Widely adopted by value investors
